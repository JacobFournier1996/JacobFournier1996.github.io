Privacy Policy

Sub-Zero Smash · Last updated October 22, 2026

Sub-Zero Smash is a game made by Jacob Fournier. This page explains what information the game uses and why. In short: the game itself doesn't ask for or keep any personal information. The ads in the game come from an advertising partner, which collects some device information to show and measure ads.

Information the game keeps

Your progress (levels, coins, stars, hats, settings) is saved only on your device. There are no accounts, and we don't run any servers that receive your game data. Deleting the app deletes this data.

Advertising

The game shows ads through Unity LevelPlay (by Unity Technologies), which may work with other ad networks to fill ads. To choose, show and measure ads and to prevent fraud, these partners may collect:

  • device identifiers, such as the advertising identifier (IDFA) only if you allow tracking, and the identifier for vendors (IDFV);
  • device and app information, such as model, operating system, language and app version;
  • your IP address and approximate location derived from it;
  • how you interact with ads, such as views and taps.

The first time ads start, your iPhone asks whether the game may track you across other companies' apps and websites. If you choose Ask App Not to Track, the advertising identifier isn't shared and you'll still see ads, just less personalised ones. You can change this anytime in Settings › Privacy & Security › Tracking.

Unity's handling of this data is described in the Unity Game Player and App User Privacy Policy, which also explains how to exercise your rights with them.

Purchases

The optional Remove Ads purchase is handled entirely by Apple. We never see your payment details; the game only learns whether the purchase was made.

Game Center

If you're signed in to Game Center, your scores and achievements are sent to Apple's Game Center so they can appear on leaderboards. This is covered by Apple's Privacy Policy.

Children

Sub-Zero Smash is a general-audience game and isn't directed at children under 13. We don't knowingly collect personal information from children.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted on this page with a new date.
